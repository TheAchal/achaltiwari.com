---
title: "Money before users is still a bet, even when the product is good"
description: "ProLearn just raised Rs 30 crore to build an AI learning companion before it has a single paying student, and that order of operations is the whole story."
date: "2026-07-26"
tags: ["ai", "edtech"]
---

ProLearn, founded this year by former Vedantu technology leader Ravneet Singh, has raised Rs 30 crore in a pre-seed round led by BEENEXT, with Eximius Ventures, Antler and a group of angels joining in. The product is a real-time, conversational AI learning companion for K-12 and competitive exam students, JEE, NEET, UPSC, CAT, built to listen, explain and ask questions back instead of playing a recorded lecture at you. The money is going toward product and engineering work ahead of a public launch. [Read the announcement](https://india.entrepreneur.com/business-news/prolearn-raises-inr-30-cr-pre-seed-round-led-by-beenext).

I want to be fair to this before I say anything else: real-time conversational tutoring that adapts to pace is a genuinely better bet than passive video, and a former Vedantu leader has earned the right to be taken seriously on what Indian exam prep actually needs.

But I have watched this exact category from the inside, building an unlaunched AI study companion of my own, and the thing I keep coming back to is sequencing. ProLearn now has to build the product, prove the conversational format actually holds a student's attention over weeks not minutes, and find out whether JEE and NEET aspirants trust an AI to talk them through a doubt at 11pm, all before a single rupee of revenue tells them if any of it works. That's a lot of conviction to fund in advance.

The comparison I'd draw is InfiNotes, which took the opposite order: ship something small and real to actual students first, let 10,000-plus users and a 12.9% free-to-paid conversion do the talking, and become profitable in five weeks on zero marketing before scaling anything. Neither order is wrong. But capital doesn't buy you product-market fit, it just buys you more time to go find it. The honest caveat here is that we won't know if ProLearn's bet paid off until it actually launches, and pre-seed press coverage is the easiest part of this whole journey.
